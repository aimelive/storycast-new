Becoming the Boss

How a personal trainer helped build a Kigali gym from the ground up, then took it over when its founders left Rwanda.

Storyteller
Yvan Munyengango
Role
CEO, Cali Fitness Rwanda
Place
Kigali
Length
About 3 min

Yvan Munyengango was introduced to Cali Fitness at a dinner, when the gym was still being set up by two brothers from Sweden. He helped build it from the ground up while working as an independent personal trainer, and over the years took on more and more of the running of the place.

When the owners decided to move back to Sweden, Yvan put his hand up to take over. In this conversation he explains how that happened, and why the handover to the existing team went smoothly: who he is did not change with the title.

Timestamps are approximate.

  1. Host: How did you get to be in the position you are in right now?

  2. Yvan: Fast forward from moving from place to place: I found a place I could call home, and that's Cali Fitness.

  3. Host: Of course, yes.

  4. Yvan: I remember being introduced by a guy called Peter. We were at a dinner, and he said these guys are actually setting up a gym. At that time, whenever anyone asked what I did, my professional introduction was that I'm a professional fitness trainer.

  5. Yvan: So he said, okay, I have friends, and he introduced me to the people who were setting up the gym. It was two brothers who set up Cali Fitness. After we set up a meeting and had a talk, I was involved in setting up Cali Fitness from the ground up.

  6. Yvan: I think that gained me a lot of trust, and they treated me like a brother. We started working together like that, but I was still an independent personal trainer and they were the gym owners.

  7. Host: So you were one of their first real personal trainers working in their gym?

  8. Yvan: Yeah.

  9. Host: Interesting. So how did you get from being an independent trainer on the floor to being the business owner now?

  10. Yvan: Eventually they wanted to branch out and do other things. For many years I was the personal trainer, slash the manager, slash I don't know what other positions that were not official. Then a couple of years back most of them moved out of Rwanda, and there was nothing left to be done except to take it over.

  11. Host: Where were they from?

  12. Yvan: They were from Sweden.

  13. Host: Before they moved back to Sweden, did they discuss it with you? Like, okay, we want to move, are you willing to take over? How did that process happen?

  14. Yvan: I think the conversation was not from their side to me. It was more from me to them, presenting my interest in running the gym. There were many people wanting to buy it and other people interested in doing things with it, so I placed my interest as well. Since I was already at Cali, it seemed like an obvious choice on their side.

  15. Host: And that's how it ended up. They relocated back to Sweden and left you the business. So how was that first year? Was there friction with the existing members and employees who were already working there, or was it just, oh, Yvan, he's our guy, we know him, so it was a smooth transition?

  16. Yvan: I would say it was smooth. Who I am never changed, regardless of whatever titles you put on my name, and that is not going to change any time soon. So it's always easy to give directions to the staff and the team without them feeling like there is a new sheriff in town.

  17. Yvan: I remember hosting a staff meeting just to announce the new changes. They received them really well and everybody was happy, because I have a good relationship with the staff.
