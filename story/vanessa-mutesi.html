An Open School for Every Child

At 16, Vanessa won Miss Geek Rwanda with an idea for an online school that gives every child their own textbooks.

Storyteller
Vanessa Mutesi
Role
Miss Geek Rwanda 2015 winner
Place
Rwanda
Length
About 6 min

Vanessa Mutesi was the youngest contestant ever to win Miss Geek, a competition run by Girls in ICT Rwanda to encourage girls into science and technology careers. Her winning idea, Rwanda Online Open School, puts textbooks, the curriculum and the school calendar online, so children in under-resourced schools are not left waiting to borrow a book.

In this interview, recorded at ITU Telecom World 2015 in Budapest, she talks about learning from your peers, getting parents involved in their children's schooling, and the two women who pushed her forward: her sister Lucy and her mother.

Timestamps are approximate.

  1. Host: We're here at ITU Telecom World 2015 in Budapest, Hungary, and I'm very pleased to be joined by Vanessa Mutesi, who won the second annual Miss Geek prize, a contest sponsored by the Rwandan government to spur girls' involvement in ICTs. Vanessa, welcome to the studio.

  2. Vanessa: Thank you.

  3. Host: Miss Geek is a funny name, but it sounds like a very worthy prize. Tell us a little about Miss Geek 2015, how it felt to win the award and what it means.

  4. Vanessa: Miss Geek was started by Girls in ICT Rwanda, an organisation that encourages girls to follow careers in science and technology. This year was quite challenging because I was going against very, very intelligent girls. Overall it was a great experience: we met people who trained us to do our public speaking correctly, and we had a platform to show our ideas.

  5. Vanessa: In a lot of countries there is very little women's involvement in ICT, so I wanted to show that girls do have a voice in that area. Winning was a bit nerve-wracking. Presenting to a room full of people is never easy, and being the youngest contestant they've had was even more pressure, because I was going against girls who were in college or in their last year of high school.

  6. Host: Because you're only 15, is that right?

  7. Vanessa: Yes. Recently 16, actually.

  8. Host: Happy birthday! So you were up against much older girls and you still won. That's brilliant. Tell us about the idea that won the prize. I believe it's called Rwanda Online Open School. What does it do, and what are the benefits?

  9. Vanessa: Rwanda Online Open School was inspired by children in Rwanda. Some low-income schools don't have enough resources, and that holds back the children's education. The platform puts all their textbooks, the school curriculum and the school calendar on a server, so no one has to borrow a textbook or wait for someone else to finish using it. You have your own access.

  10. Vanessa: Children will also be able to interact across the country, because teachers are not always going to be there. You use the people around you, your peers, to come up with answers to your problems. I want students to be proactive: not to wait for someone to do something for them, but to see what they can do for themselves, and even give back to the teacher and the school.

  11. Vanessa: It will also promote parents' involvement in their children's academic lives. Sometimes parents can't follow what their children are doing in school, and children can use that as an excuse to fall behind, or even give up on school. When your parents are involved and push you, it helps. I know my mother pushes me a lot to do better. Even though it's sometimes tough love, it helps you get further.

  12. Vanessa: The platform helps teachers tell parents if their child is doing well or not, and about community events they can get involved in, so they can do as much as possible as a community.

  13. Host: You mentioned your mother. Were there any role models, people who encouraged you to have an interest in ICTs?

  14. Vanessa: Yes, my sister Lucy. She is a big geek and she's proud of it. She has been one of my biggest motivations, because she has always pushed herself to be one of the women in Rwanda who spurs on women in ICT. And my mother, because she is a very, very strong woman. She has always inspired me to go above what I can do, to remember where I've come from, and to think about how I can help my people get to where I am, or even further.

  15. Host: Is Rwanda a nurturing environment for young people in ICTs?

  16. Vanessa: Yes, definitely. Technology is growing a lot in Rwanda, social media is a big buzz, and there are a lot of young people. I feel they treat us with a very high level of respect, because they see us as the future. They take our ideas, think about them, help us, nurture our ideas and guide us. They are doing a wonderful job of making sure we have the best we can get, so that we can also give back to our country what it has given to us.

  17. Host: I presume this is your first time in Budapest, and your first time at ITU Telecom World. What have been your impressions of the event?

  18. Vanessa: It has been a wonderful event, and I feel very blessed to be here because I've met a lot of people. It's not often you enter a space where so many countries are together, from Azerbaijan to India. Coming from East Africa, you don't often get to meet people from India. So it has been a good networking platform, and a good experience seeing what different people do and how we can help each other. Just because you're far away doesn't mean I can't help you, or you can't help me.

  19. Host: Vanessa, thank you so much for joining us in the studio today. I wish you all the best with your future career. It sounds like a very promising one.

  20. Vanessa: Thank you very much.
