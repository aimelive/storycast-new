Demanding Change With Facts

A young Rwandan social entrepreneur explains why she paused university to build a project, and a network, for youth with disabilities.

Storyteller
Bahati Satir
Role
Social entrepreneur
Place
Rwanda
Length
3:11

Bahati Satir spoke at a six-day gathering of 50 young African leaders with disabilities, organised with support from the Open Society Foundations. She starts from Rwanda's history: the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi left many people with disabilities, so almost every family is affected. Then she explains why she paused her studies to train in social entrepreneurship, and the independent project she plans to start for young people with disabilities at home.

She also introduces a pan-African youth network that will work with governments, and makes the case for advocacy built on evidence rather than shouting: the facts, she says, are already written in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.

Transcript

Download .txt transcript of Demanding Change With Facts

Timestamps are approximate.

  1. Bahati: As you know, in 1994 we had the genocide against the Tutsi, in which many people died and many people were left with one or two categories of disabilities.

  2. Bahati: So this is a serious case. Even if you are not disabled yourself, you have a family member who has a disability. We take it now as our responsibility as Rwandans in general to take these issues seriously, and to find ways in which people with disabilities can be fully integrated in the community.

  3. Bahati: I made a decision to suspend my studies for a while and go for a training in social entrepreneurship. That is where I really got to believe in myself. First of all, I got to know what I am best at, and my mentors also revealed other talents in me.

  4. Bahati: Today I am a satisfied and proud social entrepreneur from Rwanda, ready to establish my independent social project, which will address the challenges that youth with disabilities are facing in our communities.

  5. Bahati: Because I have lived it, and I have worked with many of them, I can see how best we can improve this system.

  6. Bahati: We want to make the CRPD work, because we believe in it. We believe it is a tool for us, and we should make it very effective. We are all committed to doing that.

  7. Bahati: That is why today we have decided to set up a Pan-African Youth with Disabilities Network, which will work intensively with governments and other youth in the community, so that our fellows get knowledge and insight into what the rights of persons with disabilities are, and how best they can advocate for them.

  8. Bahati: We will do it by convincing, not by shouting. There is a stereotype in the community that youth are very arrogant, that they shout. But we are not shouting. We demand, in a negotiating manner, with facts. And the fact here is the CRPD. Thank you.
